# Copywriting and microcopy

Words are the design. A layout with vague copy is unfinished.

## Voice

- Follow the design system's voice section. Without one: plain, specific, confident, human — no
  hype, no jargon the user didn't use.
- Write in the user's language and their domain's vocabulary (a clinic says "patients", not "users").
- Sentence case for UI (buttons, menus, headings) unless the system says otherwise.

## Headlines and body

- Say the specific thing: "Invoices paid in two clicks" beats "Streamline your finances".
- One idea per headline; the subhead adds the *how* or the *for whom*.
- Cut every word that doesn't change the meaning. Front-load the important words — people scan the
  first two.
- Numbers only when real; include the unit and the period ("38% fewer no-shows in Q2").

## Actions

- Buttons say what happens, verb first: "Create invoice", "Start free trial", "Save changes" — not
  "Submit", "OK", "Click here".
- The label on the button matches the result on the next screen.
- Destructive actions name the object: "Delete 3 files", with the consequence in the confirmation
  ("This can't be undone").
- One primary action per view; secondary actions shorter and quieter.

## States

- Empty states: what this area is for, why it's empty, and the one action that fills it.
  "No invoices yet — create one to get paid faster." + [Create invoice]
- Errors: what happened, why (if known), what to do — without blame and without codes as the
  headline. "We couldn't charge this card. Check the expiry date or try another card."
- Success: confirm the specific result and the next step. "Invoice sent to Dana. We'll email you
  when it's paid."
- Loading over two seconds: say what's happening ("Checking availability…").

## Form copy

- Labels are nouns ("Work email"), always visible — placeholders are examples, not labels.
- Help text says why you ask or what format you expect, before the error does.
- Mark the minority: if most fields are required, mark the optional ones.

## Don't

Exclamation marks in UI, "Oops!", "Simply", "Just", "Easily", fake urgency, lorem ipsum, and any line
that could appear unchanged on a competitor's product.
